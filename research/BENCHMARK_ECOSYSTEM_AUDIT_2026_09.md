# Communication benchmark ecosystem audit (2026-09)

**Status:** literature and artifact audit; no repositories cloned, dependencies installed, API called, or model run. Search capture: [`sources/research_communication_benchmarks_20260929.json`](../sources/research_communication_benchmarks_20260929.json).

## Decision

The project does not need another broad communication benchmark at this stage. Existing work already covers multi-turn private information, distributed algorithmic silos, and long-horizon collaboration. The open, narrower need is a controlled **representation** comparison that holds task instance, agent information, policy/schedule, model calls, and answer scorer fixed while reporting complete inference cost as well as the delivered channel.

Keep [`Private Match v0.1`](../experiments/private_match_v0_1/README.md) as the small, exact-scored first representation test. It is a synthetic one-way adaptation of the structured-record intersection family, not an MT-PingEval reproduction and not evidence of a general language result. Promote a second family only after an instance-level communication-necessity check and a full-information receiver gate.

## Evidence and fit

| Benchmark | What it contributes | Why it cannot alone settle Tacit's question | Recommended use |
|---|---|---|---|
| [MT-PingEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.24188) | Verifiable private-information games; automatic instance generation; fixed per-player communication budget divided over 2/4/8/16 turns; includes structured-record name-game as well as image and chess tasks. The authors report that extra turns often fail to help and can expose weak dialogue planning. | Its isotoken design answers an interaction-depth question. A Tacit codec claim still needs the same schedule and content opportunity across encodings, receiver-native payload counts, full repeated prompt/completion usage, setup and retry costs, and transfer tests. Name-game also needs a guess-and-check baseline and role-separated exact scoring. | Use its task taxonomy and per-player budget principle as design precedent. Prefer independently generated exact text records for local studies; evaluate MT-PingEval itself only when its task instances and execution dependencies can be reproduced and licensed. |
| [SILO-BENCH](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1354/) and its [official code](https://github.com/jwyjohn/acl26-silo-bench) | Thirty algorithmic tasks span aggregation, mesh, and global-shuffle communication complexity; the paper evaluates six team sizes and three infrastructures. It separates coordination/communication-reasoning failures and reports token profiles for prompts, shards, and messages. | P2P, broadcast, and shared-file conditions change delivery policy/topology, so their overall ranking does not isolate message representation. The paper's headline token metric is generated output tokens per round; a cost comparison still needs the complete model-call, delivered-wire, runtime, setup, and recovery ledgers. The reported evaluation used three very large models and >500 GPU-hour equivalents per experiment, outside this project's local budget. Earlier Tacit pilots also found specific n=2 Silo instances where local information was already sufficient, plus receiver tool-use failures. | Reuse selected task *definitions* only after auditing each seed for no-message Bayes/local-answer sufficiency. Freeze a single topology and schedule before changing message encoding. Do not rerun the paper's full matrix locally. |
| [AgentWorld](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31590) | A new long-horizon collaboration stress test with human-annotated tasks and a causal-action contribution metric. It highlights communication breakdown, role confusion, and plan-maintenance failures beyond short algorithmic exchanges. | Its MMO-like, many-agent interaction is much broader than a codec experiment; the proposed CCE graph construction uses model-mediated causal annotation. It cannot attribute a gain to representation without a controlled replay/ablation. | Secondary external-validity target after a representation has a stable, measured effect on exact-scored tasks. |
| [DALA](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/40182) | A learned auction policy allocates bandwidth and selects full text, summaries, keywords, or silence based on message bids. | It jointly changes who speaks, when, and what content is generated. That is a strong selection/policy baseline, not a matched fixed-payload codec. Its training/search and allocation cost must be charged. | Compare as a policy/system baseline where the task and resources permit; do not count its savings as a language-codec result. |

## Consequences for the research design

1. **Do not claim novelty for fixed-budget multi-turn evaluation.** MT-PingEval already establishes an isotoken turn sweep over verifiable private-information games. Tacit's candidate contribution must be protocol representation, receiver transfer, or complete-cost accounting beyond that work.
2. **Separate channel budget from inference budget.** Enforce and report delivered bytes and recipient-tokenizer payload tokens separately from every model request's full input/output tokens. An outgoing-message cap alone does not make repeated history or protocol instructions free.
3. **Use task-specific ground truth.** For each episode, publish a no-message prior, role-separated inputs, a full-information oracle, a strict terminal scorer, and a matched corrupted/mismatched-message control where meaningful. Exclude an episode if one agent can solve it from local data or the task prior already predicts the answer.
4. **Keep schedule fixed for representation claims.** Freeze sender/recipient, rounds, prompt scaffolding, and final-answer procedure across optimized natural language, valid JSON/schema, symbolic/code, and any learned or latent representation. A selector or scheduler changes a separate factor and needs its own costed comparison.
5. **Treat model capability as a gate, not a protocol result.** Require a receiver full-information control and reliable use of a correct message before ranking formats. Current local resource preflights have failed; these papers do not justify relaxing those gates or substituting remote inference.

## Falsifiable next prediction

If a reusable representation's advantage comes from transmitting task-relevant information more densely, its success advantage over a strong concise-natural-language baseline should grow as the **delivered channel budget** tightens, provided receiver decoding remains above the predeclared fidelity gate. The advantage should disappear when the recipient cannot decode the representation, when all candidate information fits comfortably in the natural-language budget, or when setup/decoder/inference cost dominates. Test this on fresh, paired task instances with an equal-schedule budget curve; if no representation moves the quality/cost frontier, report that result and keep the SDK protocol-neutral.

This is a prediction for the eventual model experiment, not an LLM result. No new model work is authorized by this audit; the predeclared local resource and capability gates remain in force.
